The swing was not east versus west. It was the 2024 Trump-surge counties snapping back.

Every one of Pennsylvania's 67 counties moved toward Democrats between the 2024 presidential race and the 2026 governor's race, by a mean of 22.8 points. The biggest moves were in the old industrial Democratic counties of the anthracite region and the Mon Valley, in both corners of the state.

Swing is the difference in points; positive means movement toward Democrats. The 2022 governor's race (Shapiro over Mastriano) is the other reference point.

No gradient from east to west

If the shift had been a regional story, the swing would track longitude. It does not. Across the 67 counties the correlation between swing against 2024 and longitude is 0.04, and the vote-weighted swing is +23.6 points both east and west of the Susquehanna. Where the ground moved most, and least

The counties that moved most against 2024 are the working-class counties that swung hardest to Trump in 2024: Greene, Lackawanna, Schuylkill, Carbon, Luzerne, Washington, Beaver, Fayette, Lawrence and Cambria, along with the Lehigh Valley. The smallest moves are in the rural north-central tier, where Trump's 2024 margins were already near their ceiling.

A hypothesis, not a finding

The simplest reading is that the voters who gave Trump his 2024 surge in the anthracite and Mon Valley counties did not show up the same way for a midterm with Shapiro at the top of the ticket, through some mix of staying home and voting for Shapiro. County returns cannot separate those two: a county where Trump's 2024 voters stayed home and a county where they switched look the same on this map. Two things argue for turnout doing much of the work. Turnout fell most where Trump ran best (see the turnout page), and against 2022, a midterm with no Trump on the ballot, the same southwestern counties moved only 3.7 points while the northeast moved 9.1.
