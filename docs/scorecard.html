The forecast had the right map and the wrong environment

The model called Democratic control of the Senate a shot and it happened. Its four Senate tossups were the four seats that flipped. But every competitive bin broke bluer than its probabilities said, because the model let too little of Shapiro's landslide pass through to legislative races.

The Senate outcome was the model's second most likely branch, so this is a miss of direction rather than a shock. The House was never in doubt and the model said so. The question is why the seat count landed at the top of both ranges, and the answer is in the seat-level errors.

Calibration

The bias

The model's generic legislative environment was a Democratic margin of points, with a 90 percent range of to . The seat results imply something closer to 9 to 11 points. The governor polling average the model used, Shapiro plus 23.1, matched the result (plus two-party). So the miss was not in the top-line polls. It was in the assumption about how much of a governor's margin reaches down-ballot races. The governor polls were one of several inputs to the environment, and that input subtracted a 10-point "personal vote" from Shapiro's margin and moved only halfway from the 2024 baseline; the national generic ballot, at plus 7.9, carried as much weight. Those are judgment calls that the result says were too conservative, and they are the first thing to revisit. This is a hypothesis about the model's structure, not a measurement; a full re-fit with the actual environment is the test.

What the model got right

The four seats it rated Tilt D (SD-6, SD-16, SD-24) or Tilt R (SD-40) with the Republican incumbent in danger were the four that flipped. SD-36, where a Democrat won a low-turnout 2025 special in Lancaster County, was rated Tilt R and the Republican won. In the House, of contested seats went to the side the forecast favored. The 90 percent intervals covered of contested seats, close to the 90 percent they should, so the uncertainty was sized about right even though its center was off.
