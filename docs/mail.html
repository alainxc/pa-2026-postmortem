Every county moved the same way

The shift was not concentrated anywhere. In all 67 counties the Republican mail share of the Republican vote was lower in 2026 than in 2024, and in most it was roughly half. Switch the chart to the Democratic margin among mail voters or among election-day voters to see the same uniformity: the dots sit well above the diagonal almost everywhere, which is what a statewide swing looks like at county resolution.

Read this with three caveats

Philadelphia's mail count may not be finished. Mail ballots are of Philadelphia's governor vote so far, against in 2022 and in 2024. Allegheny and Chester show smaller dips of the same kind. If late mail is still being added in the city, the statewide mail share and the Democratic mail margin will both rise.

Provisional ballots are essentially missing. They are of the 2026 count so far and were of the 2024 vote; of 67 counties report zero. Provisionals are adjudicated after election day and come in late.

The state's "precincts reporting" figure covers in-person ballots only. There is no public statewide measure of how much mail remains to be counted. So the finding on this page is stated as a hypothesis: Republican voters who used mail in 2024 mostly did not in 2026, either because they voted in person or because they did not vote. The turnout page shows the second possibility is a big part of it. Re-check this page after certification.
