How the governor's margin by district is estimated

Legislative districts do not follow county lines, and the state publishes 2026 results only by county until certification. So each district's governor margin here is its exact 2024 presidential margin (built from certified 2024 precinct returns on the 2022 district lines) plus the 2026 governor-minus-2024 president swing of each county the district touches, weighted by the legislative votes cast in that county piece. A district inherits its county's swing, so a Philadelphia or Allegheny sub-county district is assigned the whole county's shift. That is the main source of error, and it is why every number derived from this estimate is labeled as such. Use the toggle to see the same chart against the exact 2024 presidential margin instead.

Caveats

The governor estimate assigns each county's swing uniformly to every district within it. Where a county's swing was uneven, for instance between Philadelphia's river wards and its northeast, the estimate is off for the districts inside it, and the 13-point median could move by a point or two once precincts are certified. The direction cannot plausibly flip: against the exact 2024 presidential numbers, which carry no estimate, Democratic legislators still ran 10 to 12 points ahead of Harris while Shapiro ran 24 points ahead of her statewide. Counts for the three House seats inside 220 votes (HD-41, HD-143, HD-147) could change which party they land in.
