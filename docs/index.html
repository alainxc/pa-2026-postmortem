A 22-point governor landslide bought Democrats a 3-seat Senate gain and a 10-seat House gain

Pennsylvania voted on November 3, 2026. Democrats took the state Senate, widened their House majority and flipped four congressional seats. But the legislature moved far less than the top of the ticket, because Republican legislators held on to about 13 points of ticket-splitting.

Five things the returns show

1. Ticket-splitting is the story. Josh Shapiro won by points two-party. In the contested legislative races, the Democratic candidate ran behind Shapiro's estimated margin in the same district by a median of about points, and behind him in every contested Senate seat. Nineteen districts Trump carried in 2024 elected Democrats; no district Harris carried elected a Republican. That asymmetry, not the size of the wave, is why the chambers moved only a few seats. Legislators vs the top of the ticket.

2. The forecast had the right map and the wrong environment. The pre-election model gave Democrats a chance of Senate control and expected Democratic seats; they got 26. The four Senate seats that flipped were exactly its four tossups, and the one Democratic loss was the one it leaned Republican. But contested seats ran a mean of points bluer than forecast in the Senate and in the House. The scorecard.

3. Every county swung toward Democrats, by a mean of points against 2024. The pattern is not east against west. The biggest moves were in the counties where Trump surged in 2024, in the anthracite northeast and the Mon Valley alike, and the smallest were in the rural northern tier. The swing map.

4. The Republican mail vote of 2024 did not come back. Republicans cast of their presidential votes by mail in 2024 and of their governor votes in 2026. Democrats won the election-day vote statewide, by points, for the first time in the three statewide elections compared here. Mail vs in person.

5. Turnout fell most where Trump ran best. About ballots were counted, percent of the 2024 total. The Philadelphia collar kept three quarters of its 2024 vote; the southwest outside Allegheny and the rural T kept about two thirds. Turnout.

These are hypotheses with county-level evidence behind them. Precinct results, published after certification, will let the demographics and split-ticket pages test them properly. Until then every page says which level of data it shows.
